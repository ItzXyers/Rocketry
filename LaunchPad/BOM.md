# Bill of Materials — LaunchPad (v2, simplified)

Foldable ABS launch pad for a ⌀4 mm × 1000 mm launch rod, with a printed
cradle for a user-supplied metal blast deflector plate (90–150 mm, round or
square, undrilled). Vertical rod only, no tilt joint, all-printed fasteners.
Generated by `make_launchpad.py` (FreeCAD 1.1.3, headless), the CAD source
of truth; `LaunchPad.FCStd` is its saved output. All figures below are read
directly off the generated solids — see `build_verification.txt`.

**v2 supersedes v1.** At the user's request to reduce mechanism complexity,
this revision replaces v1's tapered collet + conical nut with a plain
split-collar + cross-screw clamp, replaces the two-ear clevis hinge with a
single lap-joint lug + friction pin (dropping the snap clip), and simplifies
the deflector cradle to one lip ring (dropping the inner ring + two keeper
posts). v1's numbers are summarized at the bottom of this file in case the
precision trade-off this revision makes ever needs revisiting — the physical
files in `Print Files/` are v2 only, v1's STL/OBJ were removed.

## Print settings

Same profile as v1, recovered from the rocket airframe's Bambu Studio
project files: **Bambu Lab X1 Carbon, 0.4 mm nozzle, bed 256×256×250,
Generic ABS @ 270 °C, 0.20 mm layer, Engineering Plate, auto-brim.** All 5
parts fit the bed individually with large margin (largest is the Leg at
206×48×18 mm).

- **Hub, Leg: 4 walls / 35% infill** (unchanged from v1 — these still carry
  the working loads: clamp force, leg bending/splay, stake pull-out).
- **Thumbscrew, Cradle, Pin: project default (2 walls / 15% infill).**
- **Brim: on**, all parts. **Supports: none needed.**

## Print orientation

- **Hub — rod axis vertical, as modeled.** Same reasoning as v1: makes the
  ⌀4.25 mm bore a stack of concentric perimeters, not a bridged horizontal
  hole. The clamp's cross-screw holes end up horizontal (bridged) as a
  consequence, but they're only ⌀6.4 mm and short — well within FDM bridging
  limits, unlike the old ⌀4.25 mm rod bore would have been.
- **Leg — flat on the bed**, in its own swing plane, same reasoning as v1
  (hinge-tab bore axis ends up vertical, pin load reacted as in-layer hoop
  stress rather than across a layer boundary).
- **Hinge Pin — lying down, axis in the bed plane** (shear-loaded across its
  axis; a flat on the pin makes this its natural resting orientation).
- **Thumbscrew — thread axis vertical.**
- **Deflector Cradle — flat on the bed.**

## Verification

Run headless via `make_launchpad.py` — full output in
`build_verification.txt`. New checks added for the v2 mechanism, beyond the
watertight/volume/bed/plate-overhang checks carried over from v1:

- **Screw↔hub thread interference:** 0.0 mm³ at the screw's actual installed
  position and orientation (rotated to lie along the clamp's cross-hole axis,
  not just translated the way a same-axis check would be) — 0.2 mm radial
  clearance confirmed real.
- **Screw hole clear of the rod bore:** 0.0 mm³ common volume. Flagged during
  sizing as a real risk — the cross-screw hole's radius and offset from the
  rod's own axis were close enough that an earlier offset choice would have
  had the two holes overlap, which would have let the screw poke into the
  rod passage. Checked explicitly rather than assumed clear.
- **Threaded ear material depth:** 6.60 mm of real material available where
  the thread is cut, against a 6.0 mm nominal engagement — the collar's round
  OD means material runs out near its edge, so this is verified rather than
  assumed the way a rectangular part's material depth wouldn't need to be.
- **Clamp slit open:** confirmed as an actual air gap (not silently filled by
  a boolean that failed to cut), by probing for material at the slit's own
  center point.
- **Leg-vs-hub overlap across the fold sweep: 0.0 mm³.** This check exists
  because of a real bug found while building v2 — see below.
- Rod fit, grip length, leg-leg collision, plate overhang, bed check: same
  checks as v1, all still passing (grip length is now 60 mm = 15× rod
  diameter, still comfortably over the ≥8–10× floor).

### Two real geometry bugs found and fixed while simplifying

Worth recording because both were the kind of bug that "looks fine" until
checked, not something visible by inspecting the code:

1. **Wing double-offset, disconnecting each hinge lug from the hub entirely.**
   The single-lug hinge reused the lug's own placement (rotation + full
   radial translation to R=42mm) for its connecting wing too — but the
   wing's box coordinates were already absolute-radial, so the reused
   placement doubled the offset and left the wing (and the lug hanging off
   it) floating 42mm away from where it needed to connect to the boss.
   `heal()`'s solid-count check caught this immediately (7 solids instead of
   1) rather than it slipping through as a subtly-wrong shape.
2. **Leg body genuinely colliding with the hub across the whole fold sweep
   — 722 mm³ measured, not a meshing-tolerance sliver.** The leg's hinge tab
   was repositioned to sit beside the hub's new single lug (a lap joint, not
   a clevis), but the leg's full-width (40 mm) main body still started right
   at the hinge point too, and shared the same Y-range as the hub's lug and
   wing there. This existed as a real risk in v1 as well, but v1's
   leg-vs-hub check was **dead code** — it computed the overlap volume and
   then did nothing with it, silently passing regardless of the result. That
   bug is fixed here too (the check now actually asserts on the number), and
   it's what caught this. Fixed by narrowing the leg to a short neck (sized
   to the hinge tab, not the full leg width) for its first ~55 mm, widening
   to full width only once the leg has swung clear of the hub — verified
   down to exactly 0.0 mm³ overlap across the entire fold arc, not just the
   deployed pose.

   A related, smaller version of the same *ordering* bug (cutting a hole from
   one piece, then fusing an unholed piece on top, which can partially fill
   the hole back in) was present in both the hinge lug's press-fit hole and
   the leg tab's pin hole — both fixed by fusing first, cutting the hole from
   the combined solid second.

## Corrections from the plan / from v1

- **Grip length is 60 mm** (15× rod diameter) — shorter than v1's 66 mm, but
  still well over the ≥8–10× floor; the plain split-collar clamp doesn't need
  the extra length v1's tapered-finger + solid-root arrangement did.
- **Hub standoff height is ~187.5 mm to the cradle** (was ~194 mm in v1) —
  6 mm shorter since the clamp mechanism itself is shorter. Comes from the
  same leg-triangle solve as v1 (a 190 mm leg from a hinge at R=42mm to a
  200 mm foot radius drops ~105.5 mm vertically no matter what sits above
  the hinge), so the wind-stability numbers barely move: recomputed, the pad
  overturns at roughly **9.5–10.5 m/s without stakes**, essentially the same
  thin margin v1 had. **Stakes are still required equipment, not a
  gust-only contingency** — use all three every time.
- **Deflector Cradle envelope unchanged at 190×190 mm** — the plate-overhang
  requirement (≥15 mm past the cradle's own footprint, in both directions,
  for both the largest and smallest supported plate) is independent of how
  many lip rings or keepers the cradle has internally, so simplifying those
  didn't let the overall footprint shrink.

## Simplifications from v1 (documented trade-offs, not hidden)

- **One-sided pinch clamp instead of a concentric collet.** v1's whole
  rationale for the tapered collet was that a one-sided pinch can cock the
  rod slightly, and per the pad's own governing ×1000 rule (1° of slop at
  the socket ≈ 17.5 mm of wander at the rod's tip, 1000 mm away) that
  matters more here than on a short fastener. This is the real cost of the
  simpler mechanism — not eliminated, just accepted as the trade-off asked
  for. Tightening the screw evenly and not overtightening one side reduces
  it in practice; there's no CAD check that can verify "how straight does
  this actually sit," only a physical one (square the rod against the hub
  after clamping, same check v1's docs called out).
- **Lap-joint hinge instead of a clevis.** A lap joint can rack sideways a
  little more than a two-ear clevis (which bears the leg from both faces),
  and this joint only bears load on one face instead of two. Simpler to
  print (no thin gap to keep clear of warp or blobbing) and to model.
- **One lip ring instead of two, no keeper posts.** Smaller plates (toward
  the 90 mm end of the range) now sit inside the single ⌀155 mm ring with
  real slop instead of being precisely centered, and nothing captures the
  plate's edge top-and-bottom the way v1's slotted keepers did — gravity and
  the (now taller, 5 mm vs v1's 3mm) rim are what hold it. Confirming the
  plate is seated before flying is a pre-flight check item, same as it
  already was in v1.
- **No detent/click-stop holds the legs open** (carried over from v1, where
  it was planned but never actually built either). Gravity and ground
  reaction hold the deployed position once the pad is set down; nothing
  holds it open while carried pre-deployed.

## v1 (precision collet) — summary, superseded

Kept for reference only; the physical part files for these are no longer in
`Print Files/`. v1 used a tapered 4-finger collet (⌀14→⌀11 over 40mm) closed
by a matching conical nut screwed onto a 26mm trapezoidal thread, a two-ear
clevis hinge (pin + snap clip retention), and a dual-lip-ring cradle with two
slotted keeper posts. Grip length 66mm, hub standoff ~194mm, total mass ~541g (100% solid).
Verified nut/hub thread clearance 0.0mm³, collet closure margin 4.3×. The
project isn't version-controlled, so this summary — not a diff — is the
full surviving record of that design.
