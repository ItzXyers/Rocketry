Resistor colour bands

The bay uses three resistor values. Each is drawn in both styles you are likely to have: a beige 4-band carbon-film part (5 %) and a blue 5-band metal-film part (1 %). Read from the end where the bands bunch up; the tolerance band (gold or brown) sits alone at the other end.

220 Ω R1 · buzzer gate resistor (D9 → Q1)

4-band · 5 % red · red · brown · gold 2 2 × 10 = 220 Ω, ±5 %
5-band · 1 % red · red · black · black · brown 2 2 0 × 1 = 220 Ω, ±1 %

10 kΩ R2 · buzzer base pulldown  ·  R11, R12 · battery-sense divider

4-band · 5 % brown · black · orange · gold 1 0 × 1 000 = 10 kΩ, ±5 %
5-band · 1 % brown · black · black · red · brown 1 0 0 × 100 = 10 kΩ, ±1 %

100 kΩ not used on the bay any more (R6 left with Q3 and the reed switch)

4-band · 5 % brown · black · yellow · gold 1 0 × 10 000 = 100 kΩ, ±5 %
5-band · 1 % brown · black · black · orange · brown 1 0 0 × 1 000 = 100 kΩ, ±1 %

Telling them apart

  • The 10 kΩ and 100 kΩ parts differ only in the multiplier band: orange vs yellow on 4-band parts, red vs orange on 5-band parts. Under warm light orange and yellow look alike, so check with a multimeter before soldering.
  • On 5-band parts, brown is both a digit and the 1 % tolerance band. If you can't tell which end is which, read both ways: only one direction gives a standard value.
  • Use 1 % parts for R11 and R12 if you have them. Any mismatch between the two shows up directly as a battery-voltage error (schematic item 8).
  • The colours above are approximate. Real bands vary by maker, and a meter is the final word.
