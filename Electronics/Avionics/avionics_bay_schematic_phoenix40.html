This is every electrical part on the sled v8 e-bay and how it connects. The bay is a logging altimeter and fires nothing, because D9aft deploys on the motor charge. Power is switched on the battery side: the reed switch pulls the gate of a G75P04T P-FET low, which connects BATT+ to the regulator. A divider on A0 reads the battery voltage, and the firmware logs it, prints it, and shows it on the pad's Wi-Fi page. The battery charges off the vehicle through its JST-PH lead. Numbered markers point to nine open items, listed below the drawing, to resolve before the first power-up in the airframe.

Open items before first power-up

  1. 1

    Power does not stay on once the magnet comes off

    SW1 is normally open and BOM.md lists the magnet as a ground tool that does not fly. Wired as drawn, Q3 conducts only while the magnet sits on the ARM mark. Remove it and R6 pulls the gate back up to BATT+, so the board shuts off. No document in the repo describes a latch.

    Simplest fix: use a normally-closed reed. The magnet then becomes the remove-before-flight safe (on = off, removed = powered), and nothing else in the drawing changes. Alternative: with Q3 on the battery side, a firmware soft latch also works. A free pin (D4 or D5) drives your spare 2N7000 or AO3400A from Q3's gate to GND, so firmware holds the board on once the magnet has started it. That costs one FET, one resistor and a firmware change.

  2. 2

    Buzzer type must match the firmware

    buzzer.cpp holds D9 high or low with no tone generation, so it needs an active buzzer that oscillates on its own. The sled v7/v8 notes describe the module as "KY-006-style", and a KY-006 is usually a passive transducer. Driven with DC it only clicks.

    Check: put 5 V across the module's + and − pins. A steady tone means it is active and fine. A single click means it is passive. In that case, swap in an active module or change buzzer.cpp to drive a 2–4 kHz LEDC tone on D9. If you go the tone route with a plain piezo, also fit a ~1 kΩ resistor across BZ1 (+ to −): otherwise the piezo can't discharge when Q1 turns off, and it plays quietly. Confirm the module pinout too: some KY boards leave the middle pin unconnected.

  3. 3

    Use only the reed module's S and − pins

    KY-021 boards carry a 10 kΩ resistor between + and S, with the reed between S and −. Wire only S to Q3's gate and − to GND, as drawn, and leave + unconnected. R6 (100 kΩ) is the gate pull-up. If you also connect +, the module's 10 kΩ sits in parallel with R6 and draws ten times the current while the reed is closed (about 0.4 mA instead of 0.04 mA).

    Check with a meter that the reed sits between S and −, and that the board has no LED in that path.

  4. 4

    VIN is 1 V under the Nano's spec

    Carried over as BOM item O1. U2's fixed 5 V feeds VIN, which is rated 6–21 V. Community testing says it runs, but there is no margin. C1 is fitted in sled v8, which covers the brownout case the August notes required before using the Wi-Fi interface near a loaded vehicle.

    If the board resets on SD writes or Wi-Fi bursts, suspect this before the sensor wiring.

  5. 5

    SW2 is a push button inside the sealed bay

    SW2 is now a momentary button. The firmware latches it: hold 1–5 s, then release, to turn the Wi-Fi interlock on, and do it again to turn it off. Every boot starts with it off. A tap is ignored, and a line stuck high (shorted to 3V3) never releases, so it can't turn Wi-Fi on. Once on, the AP stays up until /arm, confirmed BOOST, a second press or power-off. That draws roughly 100 mA, hours of margin on 620 mAh. Sled v8 has no place for SW2, and the bay has no wall hole.

    Procedure: with the e-bay out and powered, hold the button 1–5 s and release, check that the AP is up, then close the bay. Placement: it needs a spot in a sled v9 where your finger can reach it with the e-bay out, but nothing can press it once the bay is closed.

  6. 6

    D6 is still driven at apogee

    The firmware still pulses PIN_DEPLOY (D6) at apogee. On this vehicle nothing is connected to it: no Q2, no coil. That is harmless as long as it stays that way.

    Leave D6 unconnected. Don't reuse it for the SW1 soft latch or anything else until the deployment code is compiled out for this vehicle.

  7. 7

    Q3 is a G75P04T in TO-220: wire it by pin name, and find it a home

    The G75P04T is −40 V, −70 A, about 7 mΩ at a −10 V gate. It's rated from a −4.5 V gate with a threshold of at most −2.5 V, so the cell's 3.0–4.2 V turns it on, but below its characterized drive. At this board's current that is still a small drop. Bench-check it: with ~200 mA flowing, the voltage from S to D should be well under 50 mV. A worst-case part on a nearly empty cell drops more, one more reason not to fly below BATT_LOW_V.

    Pins: with the printed face toward you and legs down, the order is G – D – S. That isn't the S-G-D order of the box on the drawing, so wire by name: S to BATT+, D to SW_BATT, G to the R6 / SW1 junction. The metal tab is the drain (SW_BATT): cut it off or cover it in Kapton so it can't touch anything.

    Size: the body is about 10 × 15 × 4.5 mm plus the tab, and it weighs about 2 g. It doesn't fit sled v8's SOT-23 slot on top of U2. With the tab cut it is about 10 × 9 × 4.5 mm plus legs, which still needs a pocket in a sled v9 or a measured spot in the aft deck.

  8. 8

    Calibrate the battery reading once

    The ESP32-S3 ADC is factory-calibrated, but 1 % resistors and the ADC's gain error can still leave the reading 1–3 % off. That is about 0.1 V, or 10–20 % on the charge estimate near the flat middle of a LiPo curve.

    Flash chips_bench, run BATT, and measure the cell with a multimeter at about 3.9 V. Set BATT_CAL_GAIN in config.h to meter volts ÷ reported volts. The percentage comes from a generic LiPo curve, so trust the voltage more. BATT_LOW_V (3.70 V, about 12 %) flags LOW in the log, on serial and on the pad page. It never touches flight logic.

  9. 9

    Check that C1 fits its slot

    Sled v8 has a slot for a ⌀8 × 12 mm capacitor (14 mm with leads), standing in the aft deck. A 470 µF 16 V can is usually ⌀8 × 11.5 mm, but some makers use ⌀10 × 12.5 mm, which won't fit.

    Measure it. If it's ⌀10, either lay it on its side along the deck floor with the leads bent, or widen the slot in a sled v9. Respect the polarity: the stripe marks the − lead, which goes to board GND.

What changed in rev C, D and E

Power switch

  • Rev D: Q3 is an AO3401A P-FET on the battery's positive side. R6 holds its gate at BATT+, and the reed pulls it to GND. Board GND is the battery's negative lead again, and the KY-021 wires up with its normal pin labels.
  • The battery divider is tapped after Q3 (SW_BATT), so it still draws nothing while the bay is off.
  • Rev E: Q3 is a G75P04T (TO-220) instead of the AO3401A. Q1 is a BC337-40 NPN instead of the 2N7000, with R1 raised to 1 kΩ. The KY-021 uses only S and −, with R6 as the pull-up.

Battery monitor and SW2

  • R11/R12 10 kΩ divider into A0 (new firmware module battery.cpp). At 10 kΩ the ADC needs no filter capacitor, and the divider draws about 0.2 mA, only while armed.
  • C1 is your 470 µF 16 V part instead of 220 µF. More bulk capacitance only helps against brownouts.
  • SW2 is a momentary push button, latched in firmware (hold 1–5 s and release to toggle). No latching switches are needed anywhere in the bay.
  • Logged as batt_v in every FLIGHTnnn.CSV row. On /status as batt_voltage_v, batt_percent and batt_low. Shown in the serial STATE output, the bench BATT command, and a Battery row on the LaunchControl pad page.

What changed since the August diagram

Removed

  • BQ25302 USB-C charger (U3). Sled v8 deleted it and its wall slot. Charge at J1, off the vehicle.
  • GPS and its Serial1 link. D2/D3 stay reserved.
  • Servo and burn-wire channels. D9aft deploys on the motor charge.

Added or now drawn

  • SW1 reed arming through the wall, switching Q3 + R6 as a load switch (BOM item O2).
  • SW2 + R3 interlock on D7, which config.h marked "not yet in the schematic" (a button since rev C).
  • Qwiic wired as a star at the Nano, not a daisy chain.
  • Airframe interfaces: static port to U4, sound port to BZ1, ARM mark over SW1.
