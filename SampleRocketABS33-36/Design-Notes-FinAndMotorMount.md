# Design Notes — Fin Mounting & Motor Mount (SampleRocketABS33-36)

Generated by inspecting the mesh geometry directly in Blender (object list, watertightness checks, and bmesh cross-section slices), since the project has no FreeCAD assembly, drawings, or design notes on disk. Answers two "why does this look hollow" questions about `Body_Aft` (`Print Files/SampleRocketABSBody33-36_Aft.stl`).

## 1. Why is the tube wall hollow/open where the fins insert?

The fin slots are **through-the-wall (TTW) mounting slots**, not a modeling defect.

- `Body_Aft` is a thin hollow shell (a body tube), and the mesh is fully watertight — 0 open/boundary edges (5,997 edges, 3,616 polygons, all manifold).
- At each of the three fin positions (`Fin_A`, `Fin_B`, `Fin_C`), a slot is cut completely through the tube wall so the fin's root tab passes *into* the tube's hollow interior, rather than just butting against the outer skin.

**Why:** TTW mounting lets the fin root bond directly to the motor mount tube inside (see §2) instead of relying on the print's outer wall skin alone. That's a much stronger joint — important for an FDM-printed ABS airframe, where a surface-glued fin only has a thin layer-adhesion surface to hold onto and is prone to popping off or delaminating under flight/landing loads.

## 2. Why is there an open gap between the motor mount and the airframe wall?

`Body_Aft` is actually **two concentric tubes printed as one piece**: the outer airframe wall and a separate, smaller-diameter motor mount tube running inside it. They are only connected where the fin root tabs bridge the gap between them — everywhere else around the circumference, the annular space between the two tubes is open air. That open annulus is the "hollowness" being asked about.

### How this was confirmed

Cross-section slices were taken through the `Body_Aft` mesh at multiple stations along the tube's long axis (world Y), measuring the radius band(s) present at each station, relative to the tube's local center:

| Station (Y, mm) | Region | Radius band(s) found | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| ‑90, ‑80 | Near the aft tip / retainer-cap threads | single band, r ≈ 15.5–17.5 | **Single wall only** — just the airframe tube here; no inner tube yet. |
| ‑75 to ‑5 | Fin section, forward to the Body_Fwd joint | two bands: r ≈ 8.6–9.0 (continuous, gaps ≤ 5°) and r ≈ 17.5 (outer wall) | **Two full concentric tubes** — outer airframe wall + inner motor mount tube, not touching except at fin roots. |

The inner band's angular coverage was checked at each station and found continuous through the full 360° (max gap between sampled points was only 2–5°, i.e. no missing arc) — confirming it's a genuine complete inner tube, not just three isolated ribs at the fin angles.

Outer wall thickness at the single-wall aft section measures ≈ 2 mm (17.5 mm OD − 15.5 mm ID radius band).

### Why it's built this way

The three fin tabs, which pass through the airframe wall (§1) and bond to the inner motor tube, are doing the job that separate glued-in **centering rings** would normally do in a traditional kit — they center and structurally tie the motor tube to the airframe. Leaving the rest of the annulus open (rather than filling it or adding a solid disc) is a deliberate FDM-print design choice:

- **Weight** — solid plastic filling that annulus at the tail would add mass in the worst possible place for stability/altitude.
- **Print time / material** — a solid web there adds print time and filament for zero structural benefit.
- **Load path already covered** — the fin-tab-to-motor-tube bond carries thrust loads the same way a ring would.
- **Gas/pressure venting** — keeps a path open for ejection-charge pressure to act on the airframe past the motor tube.

## Caveats

- No FreeCAD assembly, drawings, or design rationale existed in the project directory — everything above was measured directly off the `Body_Aft` mesh in Blender, not sourced from original design intent.
- Motor tube inner diameter (~18 mm, from r ≈ 9 mm) was not cross-checked against a specific motor size/mount standard — verify against the intended motor before assuming clearance/fit.
- If a more traditional look/feel is wanted, additional stub webs could be added between the fin positions, but structurally the three fin-tab connections are already equivalent to centering rings.
