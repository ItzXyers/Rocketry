## Print Orientation Notes

**Fin (`Print Files/SampleRocketABSBody33-36_Fin_x3.stl`) — print flat, not on edge.**

Orient the fin with its flat face down on the bed: the 2.0 mm thickness builds up vertically in Z, and both the 50.8 mm root chord and 36.5 mm span lie flat in the XY plane. Rationale:

- FDM's weakest bond is between layers (Z). Printing flat keeps that weak plane parallel to the fin's face, so perimeters/raster run continuously along the root-to-tip and leading-to-trailing directions — the axes that carry load from aerodynamic bending, flutter, and landing impact. Standing it on the root edge instead stacks thin layers along the chord, putting the weak interlayer bond directly across the load path.
- Bed adhesion: standing on edge means only a ~2 mm-wide line touches the bed — prone to tipping and, especially with ABS, warping. A full flat face adheres far better.
- No supports needed either way, since it's a simple flat plate — flat orientation has no downside there.

Note: as modeled, the STL is **not** already sitting flat. Its native coordinate system has the root chord (50.8 mm) running along Z, span (36.5 mm) along X, and thickness (2.0 mm) along Y — the opposite of print-ready. Rotate ~90° about the fin's long axis in the slicer (so the Y-thickness axis becomes vertical) before printing all 3 copies.
