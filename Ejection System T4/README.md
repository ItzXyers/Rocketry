# Ejection System T4: CO₂ spring-striker ejector (dev build v1)

A spring-loaded striker drives a needle into an 8 g threadless CO₂ cartridge. The released gas
pressurises the recovery bay and pushes out the parachute and nose cone. A Dyneema line holds the
striker cocked, and a nichrome coil cuts the line to fire it. The cartridge and all of its support
structure form one **removable module that screws into a bulkhead from the front** (nose end).

Everything is generated by one parametric FreeCAD script. Change a parameter, re-run it, and read
the check report.

## 1. How it works

```
 z (mm, from module aft face)
 137 ┌──── cap ────┐  ◄ Dyneema line through tail cross-hole, clamped under 2 M2 screws;
     │  ▲ tail (D) │    nichrome between 2 × M2 terminal screws, over an air-gap trough
 125 ├─ T3 thread ─┤
     │   spring    │  ◄ any compression spring OD ≤ 16, ID ≥ 6.6; spacers set preload
  90 │ ▓ striker ▓ │  ◄ Ø16 × 10, M3 hardened set screw = needle (adjustable)
  80 ├── hard stop ┤
     │  plenum Ø12 │═► 4 radial vent ports (245 mm²)
  77 │   ∆ tip     │
  64 ├─ seat edge ─┤  ◄ cartridge shoulder seats on a Ø12 edge
  53 ├─ T2 thread ─┤  head unscrews → cartridge drops in
     │  cartridge  │
  11 │   ● grub    │  ◄ M6 grub from below clamps the cartridge up onto the seat
   0 └─ T1 3-start ┘  ◄ screws into the bulkhead / test base in ~1 turn
```

* **Cartridge retention.** The M6 grub screw pushes the cartridge base up until its shoulder seats
  on the Ø12 edge in the head. The cartridge is clamped between steel and the seat, so
  cartridge-length tolerance doesn't matter. The needle's puncture reaction goes into the grub
  screw and captive nut, not into a printed thread.
* **Stroke = which tail cross-hole takes the line.** The three holes give strokes of 4, 7 and
  10 mm. With 2.5 mm penetration that's 1.5, 4.5 or 7.5 mm of free run before impact.
* **Needle depth** = how far the M3 set screw protrudes below the striker face (nominal 5.5 mm =
  3.0 gap + 2.5 penetration). The striker bottoms on a hard stop, so penetration is fixed by the
  screw, not by the spring.
  * To adjust it, put a 1.5 mm hex key down the bore in the tail top to reach the set screw's
    socket.
  * Lock it with an M3 thin jam nut against the striker face.
* **Line release path.** The line leaves the tail through two 1 mm relief slots in the cap bore.
  Once cut, it pulls free along a defined channel instead of being dragged through the 0.3 mm gap
  around the tail.
* **Gas path.** Gas flows from the pierce hole into the plenum and out 4 radial ports into the
  recovery bay. Port area is 35× a 3 mm pierce hole, so the head doesn't pressurise and nothing
  needs sealing. The module sits *inside* the pressurised bay, so any leak still ends up in the
  bay. Only the bulkhead has to be gas-tight.
* **Anti-rotation.** The tail has a D-flat that rides in a D-hole in the cap. The cross-holes stay
  aligned with the line groove, and the cocking screw can thread into the tail without it spinning.
* **Removal from the front.** The body flange has two key notches. The **install tool** (two
  prongs that clear the head skirt, with a handle ring above) reaches down the Ø40 bore and turns
  the *body*, which carries the bulkhead thread. It never drives through the cap or head threads,
  which would unscrew first.

### Hardware

| Item | Spec | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Needle | **M3 × 16 DIN 914 (cone point), 45H hardened**, point ground to ~60°, with **a flat ground along the tip** | Hardened steel so the tip doesn't mushroom on the seal. The flat keeps a gas path open while the needle sits in the hole. |
| Needle nut | M3 brass heat-set insert (Ø4 × 5.7), or tap M3 directly | In the striker bottom. |
| Needle lock | M3 **thin** jam nut (DIN 439, 1.8 mm) | Against the striker face. It clears the cartridge tip by ~1 mm at end of stroke; a full-height nut won't. |
| Line clamps | 2 × M2 × 6 screws + M2 washers | Clamp the line under the washer. Adjustable, and no printed posts to snap under load. |
| Cartridge clamp | M6 × 12 set screw + M6 hex nut | The nut drops into the hex pocket from inside the bore before the cartridge goes in. |
| Terminals | 2 × M2 × 6 steel screws (self-tapping into Ø1.7) | Nichrome touches only these, never plastic. |
| Burn element | Nichrome 80, ~32 AWG (0.2 mm), 2–3 turns around the line | Bench-verify the burn current. The avionics MOSFET channel is still open (see the old T3 checklist). |
| Line | Ø0.3 mm Dyneema/Spectra braid | Melts cleanly at ~150 °C. Not Kevlar, which chars instead of melting. |
| Cocking screw | M3 × 25 (or longer) | |
| Striker mass (optional) | M6 washers over the tail, under the spring | Add mass as a test variable. |
| Pull pin (dev) | Ø1.5 music wire / nail | Goes through the same cross-hole instead of the line, for manual firing. Lies on the cap ~1.2 mm higher than the line, so stroke grows by ~1.2 mm. |
| Spring | Any, **OD ≤ 16, ID ≥ 6.6** | Cavity is 35 mm at end of stroke and 31 / 28 / 25 mm cocked (4 / 7 / 10 stroke), minus spacers. |

## 3. Choosing springs for the test matrix

Stored energy delivered over the stroke:

**E = ½ · k · (x_c² − x_f²)**, where x_f = L_free − (35 − spacers − washers) and x_c = x_f + stroke.

The spring **must still be compressed at the end of stroke** (x_f > 0), and its **solid height
must be below the cocked cavity**.

Worked example (illustrative only, not a recommendation): music wire d = 1.5, OD 14, 8 active
coils, gives k ≈ 3.2 N/mm. With L_free = 40, stroke 10 and no spacer, x_f = 5 and x_c = 15:
cocked force 48 N, **E ≈ 0.32 J**, and a ~5 g striker reaches ~11 m/s. The Wahl-corrected
stress at 48 N is ≈ 535 MPa, which is fine for music wire held cocked for hours.

**Unknown to find with this rig:** the energy and force that reliably pierce the cartridge seal.
Suggested sweep:
1. Stroke 4 → 7 → 10.
2. At each stroke, stack spacers 0 → 15 mm.
3. Then add M6 washers for mass.

Log the result as pierce / no pierce / partial. Also record the needle-tip condition after each
shot, because one needle serves many cartridges.

## 4. Before the first full print (≈30 min): coupons

1. **Thread coupons.** Male should turn into female by hand, with no slop. If they're too tight,
   raise `THREAD_CLEAR`. If too loose, lower it.
2. **Seat gauge.** This is the head section from the cartridge bore up to the striker hard-stop
   plane. Push a real cartridge in neck-first from the wide end until the shoulder seats.
   * The body must slide in freely. That checks `BORE_CLEAR_D`.
   * Measure from the gauge's top face down to the cartridge tip. **Nominal is 3.0 mm**
     (`GAP_STOP`). Set the needle protrusion to *measured gap + 2.5 mm*.
   * If it's far off (< 1 or > 6 mm), change `SEAT_D` or the `CART_*` profile and regenerate.

**Please measure one real cartridge** from the Amazon pack (B00SV61MHA): overall length, body Ø,
neck Ø, and tip height above the point where the shoulder is Ø12. Only length, body Ø and neck Ø
of the reference STL come from a datasheet. The shoulder and tip shape is an approximation, and
the seat position depends on it.

## 5. Load, cock, install

0. **Needle.** Set the protrusion (§4) with a 1.5 mm hex key through the tail top, then lock
   the jam nut.
1. **Nut.** Drop the M6 nut into the hex pocket at the bottom of the body bore and thread the grub
   screw in from below. Leave the grub screw backed off.
2. **Cock the head.** Screw the cap into the head with the spring, spacers and striker in place.
   Set the cocking tool on the cap and thread the M3 screw into the tail top. Turn it until the
   chosen cross-hole clears the cap top.
3. **Clamp the line.** Thread the line through the cross-hole so it exits through both relief
   slots, then lay it in the cap groove. Pass one leg through the nichrome coil, pull both legs
   snug, and clamp each under its M2 screw and washer. Back the cocking screw out slowly and remove
   the tool. The line now holds the striker. Check the tail sits on the line and hasn't crept down.
4. **Load the cartridge.** Drop the cartridge into the body, base first, and screw the cocked
   head on until it seats on the body.
5. **Clamp the cartridge.** Turn the grub screw in from below **until it stops, then about ⅛ turn
   more**. Don't reef on it: an M6 screw can easily crush the printed seat edge and shift the
   needle gap.
6. **Wire it.** Connect the nichrome leads (2-pin connector, e.g. JST-PH) and route them through
   the flange wire notch.
7. **Install.** Screw the module into the bulkhead or test base with the install tool. T1 is
   3-start, so it takes about one turn and barely twists the leads.

**Reload after a flight.** Unscrew the module with the install tool, then unscrew the head. Knock
out the spent cartridge. It will be cold and frosty, so let it warm first. Re-cock, re-tie with a
new line, and continue from step 4.

## 6. Safety requirements (not optional)

* **Stuck-nose overpressure.** 8 g of CO₂ is ~4.4 L at 1 atm. Sealed in a ~0.25 L bay, that is on
  the order of **15–20 bar**. At 17 bar a Ø40 × 1.5 mm printed ABS tube sees ~23 MPa hoop stress,
  **at or beyond what printed layers hold**.
  * The nose retention (friction or shear pins) must release well below that, and must be tested
    with the real cartridge (§7).
  * **Never** run a bench test with the nose taped, pinned or otherwise fixed.
  * **Never** run one in a closed tube, whether airframe section or bay simulator. Leave the end
    open or use a loose slip cap.
* **Bench piercing.**
  * Bolt the test base down.
  * Wear eye protection and gloves: the gas and cartridge reach about −78 °C with dry-ice spray.
  * The 4 ports vent radially at z 66–78, so stand clear of that plane. An open 43 mm OD tube in
    the base socket turns the blast upward.
  * Use the pull pin for spring tests. Only use the nichrome once the pierce is reliable.
* **A cocked module is armed mechanically.** Anything that cuts or unties the line fires it. Cock
  it last. Never cock it with the head off a loaded body pointing at anyone.
* **Printed threads** only carry clamp and inertia loads. Puncture force goes to steel. The bay
  pushes the module *into* the bulkhead, so T1 is loaded in compression during ejection.

## 7. Status and open items

**Geometry** (all automated in the script, both states, all 3 strokes): no interference, all
pass-throughs open, thread coupons mesh with real ridge engagement, and every wall is ≥ 1.2 mm.
See `Models/check_report.txt`.

**Not closed by CAD:**
1. Real cartridge dimensions (§4). The seat and needle gap depend on them.
2. Pierce energy and force. That is the whole point of this dev build.
3. Nichrome burn current and time, plus the flight-computer MOSFET channel.
4. Nose-retention release pressure with a real cartridge (§6).
5. Whether the needle, left in the hole, throttles the flow. If the pierce succeeds but the gas
   dribbles out, deepen the flat on the needle or shorten penetration.

**⚠️ Flight integration: this module does *not* fit the current Phoenix 40/43 layout.**
* It is **137 mm** from the aft face to the cap top (149 mm to the cocked tail), plus a 17 mm
  bulkhead. Phoenix `DESIGN.md` §7.8 has 143 mm between the nose shoulder and the anchor
  bulkhead, and that space holds the 90 mm canopy.
* Loaded mass is **~110 g**. Phoenix dry structure is ~135 g and flies on 18 mm motors.

The dev build is unaffected. For flight, one of these has to change:
* lengthen the airframe by about 150 mm;
* shorten the module (e.g. `SPRING_CAVITY_FIRED` 35 → ~20 with a stiff short spring once testing
  picks one: −15 mm);
* move to a larger vehicle.

That decision is open.
