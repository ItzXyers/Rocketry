Readiness gate. Fly only with a live igniter if all of these are true. If any is false, run the whole sheet as a dry rehearsal with a dummy load and no live igniter.
  • Igniter firing circuit is built, and FIRE_PIN / ARM_SENSE_PIN / CONTINUITY_PIN are confirmed against it (LaunchControl README, "Hardware contract").
  • ignition.py arm/fire/cutoff has been bench-tested against a dummy load (LED + resistor), with cutoff timing measured.
  • Wi-Fi name/password changed from the defaults on both the flight computer (config.h) and the Pi (config.py, network/*.conf).
  • Built rocket balanced: CG ≤ 226 mm from the tip, measured with a loaded D9, the bay packed and the line installed. Add ballast to the EbayCap if needed (~0.6 mm per g).

0 · Go / No-Go: morning of the launch

  • Fire ban (eldningsförbud) checked for the municipality today (kommun website, Länsstyrelsen, or the brandrisk / MSB app). A ban means no launch.
  • Wind forecast ≤ 3–4 m/s. Leaving the 1 m rod at about 11 m/s is below the 15 m/s target, so fly on calm days only. Drift under the canopy is about 5 m/s.
  • Site confirmed: club launch day (SMRK Högsäter), or written landowner OK. If within 10 km of Landvetter or Säve, the airport has been called and has agreed.

1 · Before leaving for the range

  • LaunchControl fired-latch cleared with scripts/clear_fired_latch.py, and no "latch already set" warning in the Pi's boot log. Re-check after any bench test today: a dummy-load test sets the latch.
  • C1 is fitted on this sled (or the transient was bench-verified). Check it, don't rely on memory.
  • Flight-computer LiPo charged, and Pi power source charged.
  • Flight-computer flash has ≥ 5 MB free (LOGS over USB or GET /logs). Old logs pulled and backed up, then deleted. Below 5 MB the self-test fails on purpose.
  • Flight-computer battery unplugged for transport. There is no power switch, so plugging in the battery powers the bay.

Pack list

  • Rocket: nose, FwdTube/AftTube with sled, fin can, motor cap
  • Klima D9-5 motors (D9-3 is too early and D9-7 too late; don't swap)
  • Igniters for the D9
  • Canopy ⌀500, 5 mm retention line, Nomex blanket / wadding
  • Tape (one wrap on each motor casing)
  • Hex keys for M5 nose screws and M3 bay screws, spare screws
  • Scale + balance edge for the CG check, ballast
  • Launch pad: hub, 3 legs + hinge pins, thumbscrew, deflector
  • ⌀4 mm steel rod, 1000 mm
  • Blast plate / board, if the ground is not bare
  • Water + fire extinguisher
  • LaunchControl Pi, firing box, leads, arm switch / key
  • Phone for the webapp; laptop + USB-C cable (serial console / log pull)
  • This checklist, pen, spare batteries

2 · At the range: prep area

Ground station

  • LaunchControl Pi powered, phone joined to its hotspot, webapp loads.
  • AP+STA concurrency (iw phy0 info) has been confirmed at least once for this Pi/kernel.

Aft section: recovery + motor

  1. Line, canopy and Nomex all fit in the bay by hand. The line runs through the bulkhead eyelet and the fin-can staple lug (no Kevlar leaders).
  2. Pack forward to aft: canopy against the bulkhead, then the line, then the Nomex/wadding over the fin-can gas port. Nomex is mandatory: the charge blows straight into the bay. A nylon/polyester line needs a Nomex sleeve at the staple end.
  3. Motor: one wrap of tape on the D9-5 casing (seals the gap that lets gas leak past to the nozzle), push it in against the thrust ring, then screw on the cap hand-tight + witness mark. The cap carries the ejection load.
  4. Fin can: push in, turn until the tab drops into the notch, push until 2 clicks. Pull gently to check it holds.
  5. About a 5 mm gap at the joint = wrong clocking. Do not fly. No clicks = do not fly. Never tape over the latch tongues (they vent the bay).

Forward section: avionics

  1. Nose off. Plug in the flight-computer battery. Listen for the self-test pass tone. A fail tone means stand down and check over USB with STATE.
  2. Nose back on with the 3 × M5 screws. From here on, handle gently: a jolt above 3 g held for 100 ms counts as a launch.
  3. LaunchControl status view shows the flight computer as reachable (/status: logger_ready true, log_free_bytes OK).

3 · Pad setup + igniter

  1. Pad on bare soil, gravel or a blast plate, never dry grass. Water + extinguisher at the pad. Spectators and cars upwind.
  2. Rod in the hub: clamp snug, not over-tight, rod square to the hub (1° of tilt is ~17.5 mm of wander at the tip). Deflector fitted.
  3. Carry the rocket to the pad and slide the lug onto the rod. It must slide freely.
  4. Rocket vertical, undisturbed.
  5. Igniter installed per Klima instructions, with the firing-circuit arm switch OPEN.
  6. Webapp continuity reads OK. If it doesn't, check the igniter connection. Never bypass the continuity gate.
  7. Webapp arm-sense reads OPEN while the switch is open. A wrong reading means the UI can't be trusted: stand down.
  8. Do not handle, bump or jar the rocket from here on.
Stand-down / reset. If the rocket is bumped hard, comes off the rod, or the result of /arm is genuinely unclear: open the arm switch, remove the igniter, power-cycle the sled (unplug and re-plug the battery, listen for the pass tone), then restart from §3 step 3.

4 · Wireless arm (re-zero the flight computer)

  1. Rocket has been vertical and undisturbed since §3 step 4. /arm takes a new pad zero (baro + accel baseline), so the rocket must be in its final position.
  2. Trigger /arm from the LaunchControl webapp.
  3. Expect the connection to drop about 0.5 s after success. An "ambiguous" result plus a dropped connection most likely means it worked.
  4. Do not retry /arm. A successful arm turns the radio off until the next power-cycle. If the outcome is genuinely unclear, use the stand-down box above.
  5. Confirm arm success from the last status the webapp captured. Don't proceed on "probably fine".

5 · Final arm + fire (igniter channel)

  1. All personnel at the range's safe distance. RSO gives the range clear.
  2. Designated operator only closes the physical arm switch.
  3. Webapp arm succeeds (needs arm-sense closed and continuity OK). If it fails, stand down and fix the igniter circuit. Never fire on a failed arm.
  4. Arm window is 15 s. If the hold runs longer, re-arm. Don't just press fire again.
  5. Countdown per range procedure, then FIRE.
  6. No ignition within a few seconds = misfire. The fire channel is one-shot. Open the arm switch and follow the range misfire wait before approaching the pad.

6 · Flight, recovery + data

  1. Watch the flight. Note the apogee time and when ejection happens (video if possible).
  2. Recover. Check the motor is cool. Note damage to the fin can, latches, line and canopy.
  3. Open the arm switch on the firing box. The LaunchControl fired-latch now reads "set". That is expected. Clear it (scripts/clear_fired_latch.py) only before the next flight.
  4. Pull the log. Power-cycle the sled (the Wi-Fi turned itself off at launch). The restart starts a new, empty log, so the flight is usually the second-highest number.
  5. Wi-Fi: join the flight computer's network, curl http://192.168.4.1/logs, then curl -OJ "http://192.168.4.1/log?n=NN". Or USB: LOGS, then DUMP NN (save what's between BEGIN/END).
  6. Back up the CSV in two places (laptop + phone/cloud) and check that it opens. Only then free the space with DEL NN.
  7. In the CSV, find APOGEE_CONFIRMED / APOGEE_TIMEOUT and the ejection accel spike. Expected: ejection ~0.5 s after apogee. A large gap means the motor delay is wrong for this rocket. Fix it before the next flight.
  8. Unplug the flight-computer battery before transport.
